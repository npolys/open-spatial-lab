What shipped: render-adapter.mjs (the engine-agnostic interface), three-render-adapter.mjs (the concrete three.js implementation), and spatial-math.mjs (pure matrix math, no engine dependency). Five files' scene-construction code now runs through the adapter instead of importing THREE directly — scene.js, wow-scene.mjs (including the matrix-decomposition fix we designed together), canonical-world-content.js, portal-render-controller.mjs's mesh-sync functions, and app.js's fabric-transclusion/debug-ring builders. Every step was verified against the real verify-demo.mjs E2E suite (lobby, avatar equip, two portal crossings, toolbar responsiveness, WoW contract validation) in the Ubuntu WSL environment — all passing, zero behavior change.

What Phase 1 correctly left alone, and why — this is the more important outcome of this session: three separate categories of code turned out not to belong in a "mechanically extract construction code" pass at all, and forcing them in would have been the wrong abstraction:

movement-camera-controller.mjs → camera control and raycasting are engine-native concerns (confirmed via tracing: real THREE.Raycaster needs real meshes, camera.lookAt() needs real Vector3). Belongs to Phase 3, against each engine's own viewpoint/picking API.
portal-spatial-preview.mjs and the aperture-preview code in app.js/portal-render-controller.mjs → the render-target/shader/clipping-plane subsystem. Belongs to Phase 0's spike and Phase 6's PortalRenderer interface.
Avatar files → per your decision to keep them decoupled toward H-Anim, they become ThreeVrmHumanoidProvider's unchanged internals in Phase 5, not RenderAdapter consumers.
